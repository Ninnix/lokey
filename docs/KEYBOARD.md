# Using the keyboard

Commands and repository paths below are relative to the repository root.

## Gestures

- The number row is always visible. Hold a digit for its US Shift symbol.
- Letter keys use US International accent variants, with the first variant printed on the key. Hold and slide for more: `a → à á â ä ã å æ ā ą ă`, `e → è é ê ë ē ė ę ě`, `n → ñ ń ň ņ`, `c → ç ć č`. Polish, Czech, Romanian, Hungarian, Turkish and Baltic variants are also included (`ą ă ě ı İ ő ű ų ş ļ ņ`). Shift and caps lock also apply to accents. Letters without common variants have no duplicated symbol shortcut; punctuation remains on the number row and ?123 pages.
- Tap period for `.`. Flick it right for `?`, left for `!`, or up for `,`, without waiting. Holding it opens only `! , ?`. Swipe up on a letter or digit for its printed alternate; hold and slide for the complete accent list.
- Hold or slide across space to move the cursor inside the active text field. It stops at the beginning and end, including in empty fields, without moving focus to other controls. Hold Enter to record; release to stop and transcribe.
- While holding Enter, slide up to lock recording. Release your finger and keep talking; the same Enter key becomes Stop. Tap it to stop and transcribe. Slide left while holding, or tap Cancel in the waveform row, to discard audio. The suggestion row shows live microphone levels and a timer while recording.
- Tap Shift to cycle off → one uppercase letter → caps lock → off. Active Shift and caps lock are red. Hold Delete for repeat. Hold ?123 for Clipboard, Translate, and Layout; Android’s system keyboard switcher remains available.
- Hold comma for a small emoji menu.
- Hold the thin handle above predictions for 400 ms, then drag up/down to change keyboard height live between 85% and 120%. Release to save; a cancelled drag restores the prior height.
- After three seconds without input, Enter becomes slightly wider over 180 ms. Letter rows stay fixed. Touching a key restores its standard size after release; no target moves beneath a held finger. Recording/resize gestures suspend idle expansion.
- The spacebar shows “Hold for cursor”; the footer slogan and its reserved blank strip are removed without shrinking the keys. Android’s own keyboard switch button remains unchanged. No settings button is added.
- Feel → Swipe to type enables optional glide input (off by default). Slide across letters and lift for a word. Shape matching combines the detected language’s vocabulary with local model scores. Alternate words stay in predictions until typing continues. Short upward accent flicks, held accents, space-cursor movement, and voice gestures remain available. Swipe input respects one-letter Shift and Caps Lock. Stale editor results are rejected and consecutive swipes are queued.

## Switch sound and feel

Feel offers eight recorded profiles: Cherry MX Red (linear), Gateron Brown (subtle tactile), Cherry MX Brown (firmer tactile), Cherry MX Blue (clicky), Holy Panda (strong tactile), Kailh BOX Jade (click bar), Razer Green (bright clicky), and Topre 45g (soft electrocapacitive dome). Select & preview plays a short sequence; the preview pads respond separately to press and release. Sound volume, release sound volume, haptic strength, and return haptics are independent controls. Silent mode silences the recordings.

Cherry MX Red bundles 86 actual per-key press/release samples from the MIT-licensed Mechvibes recordings. The other seven switch profiles are also bundled and work offline immediately. Cherry MX Red is the default on first installation. The other seven recordings add about 0.23 MB of audio to the APK (0.66 MB for all eight). SoundPool decodes these ahead of typing; there is no waveform synthesis or file access in the keypress sound path. Per-key Red sounds distinguish letters, digits, space, Enter, Backspace, and modifiers. Android's LOW_TICK, TICK, and CLICK primitives form distinct switch-specific haptic profiles when supported. Phones with optimized presets but missing primitives use native TICK, CLICK, and HEAVY_CLICK effects, with Light, Balanced, and Firm weight choices. Other devices use short amplitude envelopes. The phone cannot reproduce physical key travel or spring resistance.

Gateron Brown is a separate recorded tap from the WhatPulse switch sound board, bundled with the APK. It has no separate release recording. Credits are available in Feel and in `app/src/main/assets/switches/CREDITS.txt`; the bundled recording license is beside it. `tools/prepare-switches.py` documents the sample slicing process and uses the original Mechvibes timing maps with a development-time ffmpeg conversion.

## Speed test

Enable **Speed test** using the switch below Try it’s typing box. The same box shows the 47-word opening passage of Dante's Inferno I, in Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's 1867 English translation ([source](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1004)), as faded text. Typed characters fill the passage in; mistakes use the accent color. Letters, spaces and punctuation each count as a hit or miss. Consecutive hits raise the multiplier every ten characters, up to ×4, with points and a combo meter inside the box. Editing earlier text cannot farm hits. The timer starts on first input and freezes after all character positions are scored. WPM counts correctly matched characters divided by five; accuracy measures first-attempt characters. Prediction taps and inserted words are tracked, including multi-word suggestions. Voice or paste marks a practice run. A completed eligible run needs at least 90% accuracy to become a personal best. Character-based records use a separate key from older word-scored records.

Records stay on the phone. Share result opens Android's share sheet with a generated 1080×1350 PNG; the image is exposed through a read-only, URI-granted provider. No account, leaderboard, server, or new Android dependencies are used. `artifacts/speedtest-scorecard-preview.png` is a synthetic preview from the test, not a measured typing record.

`tools/device-test.sh multilingual` compares language detection, partial-word completions, next-word suggestions, typo candidates and prefix-cache timings across installed models. `correction` tests real Android editor replacement and undo, stale results, addresses, cursor moves and disabled correction. `speed` tests scoring, combos, misses, timing, prediction counters, practice mode, restart and PNG sharing. `artifacts/oneplus-multilingual.json` contains the device comparison: 14 short cases in Italian, Spanish, French and English. Qwen and OneJev included the expected word in all 14; LFM did so in 12. Median immediate-vocabulary timing was 44–50 ms, and median warm end-to-end model results were 101 ms (LFM), 142 ms (Qwen), and 150 ms (OneJev), including debounce. These fixtures are a regression check, not a broad language-quality or typing-speed evaluation. Earlier seven-English-prompt timings above used the original predictor.

`python3 tools/ui-prediction-test.py` verifies the installed IME in Lokey’s own editor: Italian, Spanish, French and English typos corrected on space and restored by Backspace. Each phone interaction is guarded against another app becoming foreground. Results are in `artifacts/oneplus-live-prediction.json`.

`tools/device-test.sh interaction` checks idle expansion, fixed touch targets, direct resize/save/cancel, swipe on/off, consumed Shift, new-sentence capitalization, Caps Lock, accent flicks, held accents, and space-cursor gestures. `flow` evaluates noisy swipe paths in four languages including accented words. `imeflow` injects guarded real touch paths into Lokey’s own editor to verify word commits, consecutive swipes, immediate subsequent taps, punctuation spacing, and sentence-level suggestion acceptance. `sentence` checks contextual punctuation and next-word insertion semantics before a trailing space. Keyboard-only captures are saved by the interaction checks.

## Appearance

The sun/moon button changes the app appearance independently of the keyboard. It plays a short real light-switch recording by VSokorelos (CC0), with slightly different on/off pitches; silent/vibrate modes and the system sound volume are respected. Credits are in `assets/ui/CREDITS.txt`.

Feel → Appearance & keyboard themes offers the default charcoal preset, a white preset with orange Enter and active Caps, the bundled Great Wave image supplied by the user, and custom/image styles. The orange is a saturated `#FF5A00` screen approximation inspired by [Computer–1’s RAL 2004 finish](https://teenage.engineering/products/computer-1) and [rabbit r1’s Luminous Orange](https://www.rabbit.tech/newsroom/introducing-r1), rather than a calibrated reproduction of physical paint. The color editor has exactly three palette controls: Background, Keys, and Accent. Keys applies a solid fill to all normal keys, including Space, inactive Shift, Backspace and Numbers, and clears previous row/individual overrides. Enter and active Caps keep the Accent color. Selecting a built-in theme resets color overrides; Light restores white normal keys. Each accepts RGB sliders or a hex code. Tap any key in its preview for a per-key override, or reset individual overrides to the palette.

Choose image imports a picture through Android’s document picker, decodes it off the typing thread, bounds its longest edge to 1600 pixels, and stores a private PNG. A single image spans the rounded keycaps; it is not repeated per key. Key labels choose contrasting text, while action keys retain the selected accent and individual overrides remain available. No image upload or external service is involved. The generic imported-image test uses a synthetic gradient fixture. The Great Wave preset and its render use the actual image supplied by the user.

`tools/device-test.sh themes` checks the sun/moon app toggle, recorded sound playback/silent-mode handling, orange action keys, per-key persistence, bounded image decoding and a continuous image across keycaps. The color field disables prediction while editing hexadecimal values.

Image import also derives matching background, key, and accent colors from a bounded color histogram. All three remain editable; Match colors to image restores the suggested palette. Great Wave uses a curated three-color palette: neutral paper background, pale foam keys and a muted sea-blue accent. Its colors can be customized without changing the original preset. Image labels have a subtle contrasting shadow for readability over the artwork.

Feel places Keyboard layout first, followed by Appearance & keyboard themes and the horizontal switch row. Switches are illustrated from above with their corresponding red, brown, or blue stems; selection still previews the real recordings. Appearance has a top-left Back to Feel control.

The additional recordings are bundled from the WhatPulse sound board, which credits their original open keyboard-sound projects. Cherry Brown, Cherry Blue and Holy Panda supply three press takes and a release each; BOX Jade has one press take; Razer Green supplies three press takes, converted from PCM32 to PCM16 for Android SoundPool compatibility; Topre supplies four press takes and a release. The PCM16 update passed playback checks on POCO F3, and the user confirmed that the interference disappeared. Alternate takes rotate naturally, with playback gain adjusted for the source levels. The files are preloaded off the typing thread and remain local afterward. Very short MPEG-1 recordings are decoded once to a local WAV through Android’s standard decoder, avoiding the four-frame minimum in Android 13’s MP3 extractor. Silent Red was researched but no verified reusable recording was found; the eighth option is honestly labeled Topre, rather than a filtered Red relabeled as Silent Red.

Model downloads use persisted Android background jobs, with progress bars only in the app. No notifications or notification permission prompts are used. Downloads continue across page changes and leaving the app; Android can pause background jobs and resume their partial files later. Cancel remains available on the active model control. Switch cards show type and feel; all eight are available without downloading. Model and switch selection updates the existing views and preserves scroll position. Silent / Do Not Disturb mode continues to mute switch recordings, with an explicit status in Feel.

Each profile defines its own press/return haptic composition when primitives are supported. The OnePlus continues to use optimized tick/click/heavy-click presets grouped by switch weight, avoiding its problematic short-step waveform playback. `tools/device-test.sh recordings` verifies eight distinct bundled source recordings; `feedback` checks decoding and playback for all eight.

Keyboard themes are shown as miniature keyboard previews for Dark, Light, Great Wave, and Your image. The separate Custom Colors preset is removed: any starting theme can be edited. Your image always opens the document picker; cancellation preserves the current theme. Row controls color the number row, each native letter row, and the spacebar row. Individual key overrides take precedence, while Enter and active Caps retain their accent unless explicitly changed. The color picker offers sixteen ready-made swatches before RGB/hex refinement.

`tools/device-test.sh completion` checks contractions, complete words outside the dictionary, and the reported Costa Azzurra sentence. The correction tests record accepted corrections and unchanged unfamiliar terms separately; incorrect replacements fail. They also check that valid terms, names, and acronyms remain unchanged. Voice cancellation is limited to an active speech job and cannot cancel text prediction. `tools/native-word-test.c` checks UTF-8 and word boundaries without loading a model; it can be compiled as C11 with AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer.

## Clipboard and translation

Hold ?123 and choose Clipboard or Translate. Clipboard shows copied text directly: tap to paste, pin to keep, or delete a clip. Recent clips expire after one hour; pinned clips persist. Android copy actions populate history while Lokey can access the clipboard. Sensitive clips and private fields are excluded.

Translate provides a source editor above predictions. Type, use suggestions, swipe, or dictate into that box; the active text model translates automatically into the external app’s text field. Selected external text becomes the initial source and only that selection is replaced. Without a selection, translation starts with an empty draft at the current caret. Moving the external cursor pauses insertion until Retry, so subsequent results cannot replace unrelated text. Closing the mode keeps the current translated text.

Choose a target with Auto → language; the searchable picker lists Lokey’s 39 supported speech-model languages. It hides the suggestion row to give the language list more space, and its search field has a blinking cursor. Input language is detected by the model. Either LFM 2.5 or Qwen 3.5 must be installed. Prediction, autocorrection and translation use the same resolved text model; an LFM-only installation does not require Qwen. Translation runs locally after a short typing pause. The catalog follows the voice models; character availability and model quality are shown separately. Device fixtures check English, Italian, Spanish, French and Japanese, plus cancellation and guarded editor updates.

## Keyboard layouts

Feel → Keyboard layout shows miniature keyboard previews in eleven families. Automatic follows the device language until a manual variant is selected; it can be restored at any time. Hold ?123 → Layout to change the same selection while writing, then use Back to return to the editor.

- QWERTY: Accent menus, Intl dead keys, Spanish ñ, Portuguese ç, Turkish letters/casing, and Vietnamese Telex.
- AZERTY and QWERTZ: Latin accent alternatives remain available by hold or upward flick.
- Cyrillic: Russian and Ukrainian variants share held alternatives, including ё, є, і, ї and ґ.
- Arabic script: Arabic and Persian variants share language-specific letters and native marks. Persian includes a half-space key.
- Greek: native keys with held tonos and dialytika alternatives.
- Hindi: InScript, Roman transliteration, and Bolnagri. InScript exposes ृ on a quick upward flick of र; held forms include ऋ. Roman transliteration converts words using an offline lexicon and a deterministic composer, with spaces preserved between accepted words.
- Korean: Dubeolsik keys compose Hangul syllables, compound vowels and final consonants as you type.
- Japanese: Romaji becomes hiragana, with kana/kanji suggestions; Shift starts katakana. Backspace edits the composing text.
- Chinese & Cantonese: Pinyin for simplified Chinese, Jyutping for Cantonese traditional characters, and Cangjie 5 / Sucheng shape codes. Jyutping accepts optional tones: neihou or nei5hou2 → 你好. Shape keys show radical hints. Apostrophes disambiguate boundaries; Space accepts without an extra word space.
- Thai: Kedmanee, including its fourth native row and shifted characters.

The number row remains visible in every layout. Hold digits for US symbols and, where applicable, native digits. Shift is consumed after one character; a second tap locks it. Conversion candidate pages advance using the prediction arrow; Space or Enter accepts the first candidate on the visible page. All exact shape-code candidates remain reachable, including rare Sucheng characters. All conversion dictionaries ship with the app, independently of downloaded language models; source hashes and licenses are bundled. `tools/fetch-input-sources.py` fetches the pinned sources listed in `app/src/main/assets/input-method-sources.json`; `tools/prepare-input-methods.py` rebuilds the Cantonese/shape/Hindi tables. Vietnamese Telex supports structural accents, tones, đ, and repeated-modifier escape sequences. Bengali, Hebrew and Urdu layouts are excluded as requested.

Latin languages retain automatic detection, prediction and guarded autocorrection. Other scripts use Qwen text completion when installed, with script and layout hints; native syllable/conversion input does not depend on neural prediction. This does not imply equal prediction quality in every language. Glide input is currently available for Latin layouts without a composing input method.

`tools/device-test.sh layouts` checks all keymaps, consumed Shift, automatic locale choices, script routing, composing engines, and Chinese/Japanese candidates. `layoutpicker` checks app selection and both appearances; `layoutui` exercises the actual IME in Lokey’s own editor. `speech` benchmarks the installed speech backends against local audio fixtures. Screenshots and machine-readable OnePlus results are under `artifacts/`.

On the attached OnePlus, Android currently rejects system-wide injected touch events. The interaction check dispatches guarded events directly to Lokey’s own View; live typing and layout selection use accessibility actions against Lokey’s editor and IME. The results identify the direct event delivery path.
